# Interviewing about a personal, career or life decision

Here the user is both the customer and the decision maker. The aim is to find what they actually
value, then test the options against it. Don't push them towards an answer you'd prefer.

## Ground to cover

- **The real question.** Is it "should I do X?", "X or Y?", or "how do I do X?" Often the stated
  choice hides a different one (e.g. "which job?" is really "how much risk can I take this year?").
- **What matters.** Ask them to rank three to five things: money, time, growth, people, location,
  security, meaning. Then check the ranking against what they've actually chosen before.
- **Deadline and reversibility.** When must they decide? What happens if they wait? How hard is
  each option to undo? Easy-to-undo choices deserve less deliberation.
- **Options they haven't named.** Is there a third option, a smaller test, or a way to delay?
- **Constraints.** Money, obligations, health, other people affected. Use `my-context` goals.md,
  and only ask what isn't there.
- **What they're afraid of.** Name the worst realistic outcome of each option, and how they'd
  recover from it.

## Good probes

- "Imagine it's a year from now and you chose A. What's the most likely thing you'd regret?"
- "If a friend described this exact situation to you, what would you tell them?"
- "What would you do if you knew nobody would judge the choice?"
- "Which option are you hoping I'll recommend?"
- "What's the smallest step you could take this week to learn more before deciding?"

## Care

- Ask, don't lecture. Respect that some things are private: offer to skip any question.
- For health, legal or money questions with real stakes, say when they need a professional and
  what to ask them.
- For a decision, Success in the brief means "how I'll know this was the right call", plus a date
  to check.
