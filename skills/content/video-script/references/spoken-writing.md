# Writing for the ear (and not sounding like an AI)

Read this for the read-aloud pass. A script is spoken, not read. It must sound like the creator
on a good day, explaining something to one friend.

## Order of priorities

Accurate → clear → specific → in their voice → stylish. Never trade an earlier one for a later one.

## Spoken rules

- **Short sentences**, but not all the same length. One idea per sentence.
- **Contractions and plain words**: "it's", "you'll", "use" not "utilize".
- **Say it to one person**: "you", not "you guys" or "viewers".
- **Simplify the words, not the ideas.** Keep jargon the viewer already uses (for developers:
  "API", "repo", "deploy"); explain jargon they don't, in one breath, the first time.
- **Numbers the way people say them**: "about forty bucks a month", not "$39.99/month".
- **No reading lists aloud**: more than three items → show them on screen and say the one that matters.
- **Mark emphasis and pauses sparingly** with *italics* and `(pause)`, only where it changes meaning.

## Specific beats vague

Climb the ladder as far as the facts allow:

1. Vague: "AI tools can save you time."
2. Specific: "Cursor saved me time on tests."
3. Concrete: "Cursor wrote 30 unit tests in four minutes."
4. Lived: "Cursor wrote 30 tests in four minutes. Six of them tested nothing. Here's how I caught it."

Level 4 is what only this creator can say. Unknown facts become `{{placeholders}}`, never guesses.

## AI tells to remove

If a line could appear in any AI-written script, rewrite it. Common tells:

- **Hype words**: game-changer, revolutionary, insane, mind-blowing, unlock, supercharge, unleash,
  seamless, cutting-edge, "the future is here".
- **Filler openers and closers**: "In today's video", "let's dive in", "without further ado",
  "buckle up", "in conclusion", "and that's a wrap".
- **Fake contrast**: "It's not just X, it's Y" / "This isn't about X. It's about Y." Use it only
  when X is something people actually believe.
- **Colon reveals and drumrolls**: "Here's the thing:", "The secret?", "The result? Mind-blowing."
- **Inflated significance**: "This changes everything", "a whole new era", "nothing will ever be
  the same".
- **Hedging stacks**: "might potentially be able to", "it's worth noting that".
- **Rule-of-three by reflex**: "faster, smarter, and more efficient".
- **Borrowed authority**: "experts say", "studies show", with no source.
- **Performed honesty**: "I'll be honest with you", "real talk".
- **Engagement bait**: "comment YES if you agree", "you won't believe number 3".

## Don't over-correct

Removing tells can create new ones. After the pass, check you didn't:

- Turn everything into choppy three-word fragments.
- Ban every dash, list or contrast; used with purpose, they're fine.
- Add fake quirks ("ngl", random slang) the creator doesn't use.
- Make it sound like someone else. Compare against the voice samples in `creator.md`.

## Learning the voice over time

When the creator edits a script, note what they changed. One edit is an observation. If the same
kind of edit happens three times (always cutting a phrase, always simplifying a type of line),
propose adding it to the Voice section of `creator.md`.

## The read-aloud test

Have the creator (or yourself, by reading slowly) say the script out loud at speaking speed:

- Any line they stumble on → rewrite it shorter.
- Any line they'd never say in real life → rewrite it in their words.
- Any stretch longer than ~10 seconds without something changing on screen → add a visual or cut.
