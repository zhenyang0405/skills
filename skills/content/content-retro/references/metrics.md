# Reading the numbers

Read this in steps 1-3 of `content-retro`. Menu paths drift; if one has moved, tell the user what
the metric is called and let them find it.

## Where each number lives

| Platform | Where | The numbers that matter |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube long-form | Studio → Content → the video → Analytics. Tabs: Reach, Engagement, Audience | Impressions, impressions click-through rate (CTR), average view duration, average percentage viewed, the audience retention graph (Engagement tab; exportable as CSV), returning vs new viewers, subscribers gained |
| YouTube Shorts | Same place, for the Short | **Viewed vs swiped away**, average percentage viewed (can exceed 100% with replays), shares, subscribers gained. Ignore raw views: a view counts the moment it starts |
| TikTok | The video → ⋯ or "More insights" (needs the analytics/creator tools on) | Average watch time, watched full video %, retention graph, traffic source (For You vs profile vs search), shares, saves, new followers |
| Instagram Reels | The Reel → View insights (needs a professional/creator account) | Reach (and % non-followers), average watch time, skip rate if shown, **sends/shares**, saves, follows |

Ask for screenshots or a CSV export; read them carefully and say which numbers you couldn't see.

## The four-rung ladder

Diagnose in this order. The **first broken rung** is the problem; fixing a later rung won't help.

1. **Was it shown?** Impressions (YouTube), reach or views from the feed (others). Very low after
   48 hours usually means the first test audience didn't react (look at rung 2), or, on YouTube
   long-form for a new channel, simply that it's new. Not a reason to delete.
2. **Did they stop?**
   - Long-form: CTR. Low CTR with decent retention = **packaging problem** (title/thumbnail), not
     a video problem.
   - Short-form: viewed vs swiped away / skip rate / retention in the first 3 seconds. Low = **hook
     problem**: the first frame, the first line, the on-screen text.
3. **Did they stay?** The retention graph:
   - **Early drop** (the first 30 s of long-form, first 3-5 s of short-form): the hook didn't match
     the packaging, or the intro was slow.
   - **Cliff**: a sudden steep drop at one moment. Find what was on screen and being said there
     (a tangent, a long setup, a sponsor read, a topic switch with no signpost).
   - **Slide**: a steady decline through the middle. Pacing: too long, too little changing on
     screen. Fix by cutting.
   - **Bumps up**: people rewound or rewatched. That moment is gold; make more of it.
4. **Did they act?** Shares/sends, saves, comments, follows/subscribers per 1,000 views. High
   retention with no action = useful but not remarkable; a stronger payoff, opinion or CTA helps.

## Compare against the creator's own median, never internet benchmarks

Blog posts quote "good CTR is X%" and "aim for Y% retention". No platform publishes these, and they
vary by niche, length and audience. Instead:

- Keep a running median per platform and format (Shorts vs long-form never mixed; TikTok vs
  Instagram never mixed).
- Express each video as a multiple of its median: 0.5× = underperformed, 1× = normal, 2× or more =
  a winner worth studying.
- Note **sample size** next to every conclusion. Under 5 videos on a platform: everything is noise;
  report numbers but draw no conclusions. 5-9: early signals. 10+: patterns you can act on.

## When to look

- Short-form: a first look at 48-72 hours, the real read at 7 days.
- Long-form: first look at 48 hours (CTR, early retention), the real read at 7 and 28 days; search
  videos keep growing for months.
- Don't check hourly. Early numbers swing wildly and lead to bad changes.

## Turning a diagnosis into a test

- One change per test, so you know what caused the result (e.g. "same format, but result-first
  hook").
- Write the test before posting: hypothesis, the one change, the metric that will tell you, the
  date to check.
- Need 3 or more videos showing the same result before calling it a pattern; 4+ before changing the
  strategy in `creator.md`.
