# Platform primer: YouTube, Shorts, TikTok, Instagram Reels

For someone who has never posted. Read this when choosing platforms, explaining how distribution
works, or when the user asks "how does X work?".

**These facts drift.** Checked November 2026 against official help pages where they exist and
reputable trade press where they don't. Before telling the user a limit, a payout rule or a
"the algorithm wants" claim, check the platform's own help page. Never quote an "average retention
is N%" figure from a blog: no platform publishes one. Compare a video only to the creator's own
earlier videos.

## The one idea that explains all four

None of these platforms shows a new video only to your followers. Each one shows it to a small test
group of people likely to be interested, watches what they do, and widens the audience if they
react well. So a channel with 0 followers can get views on its first video, and a channel with
100k followers can flop. What each test group does decides everything:

1. **Did they stop?** (didn't swipe away, or clicked the thumbnail)
2. **Did they stay?** (watched most of it, or rewatched)
3. **Did they act?** (shared, saved, commented, followed, watched another video)

Every skill in this pack is built around those three questions: hooks make people stop, structure
makes them stay, and a real payoff makes them act.

| Shape | Horizontal 16:9 | Vertical 9:16 | Vertical 9:16 | Vertical 9:16 |
| Length | Any; 8-20 min is typical for tutorials and explainers | Up to 3 min | Up to 10 min recorded in app, longer by upload | Up to 3 min |
| How people find it | Home feed and suggested videos (thumbnail + title), **search** | Swipe feed | For You feed, **search** (strong for "how to") | Reels feed, Explore, DMs from friends |

| Lifespan | Months to years; search videos keep getting views | Mostly days, sometimes revived | Mostly days | Mostly days |
| Best at for a tech creator | Depth, tutorials, trust, search traffic, income | Reach, testing hooks, pointing people to long-form | Reach, younger audience, search | Reach through shares, your network, personal brand |

## YouTube (long-form)

- **What decides success:** impressions → click-through rate (CTR) on the thumbnail and title →
  how long people watch (average view duration, the retention graph) → whether they come back.
  YouTube says it optimizes for viewer satisfaction, which it measures with surveys as well as
  behaviour, so clickbait that disappoints hurts the next video.
- **Title + thumbnail are one unit.** They're designed before the video is filmed (see
  `video-script`). YouTube Studio's A/B testing can try up to three titles and/or thumbnails and
  picks the winner by watch time.
- **Search is a real advantage for tech/AI.** "How to run Llama locally" is searched every day.
  A tutorial that answers a searched question can get views for years with no subscribers.
- **Where the numbers live:** YouTube Studio → Analytics, and per video → Analytics → Reach
  (impressions, CTR) and Engagement (the audience retention graph, which can be exported).
- **Money:** the YouTube Partner Program has tiers based on subscribers plus watch hours or Shorts
  views. The thresholds change: look them up on YouTube's help page when it matters. For a new
  tech creator, sponsorships, affiliate links, consulting and job offers usually arrive before
  ad revenue is meaningful.

## YouTube Shorts

- Vertical videos up to 3 minutes, in their own swipe feed. Since March 2025 a view counts the
  moment a Short starts or replays, so **view counts on Shorts are inflated; don't celebrate or
  despair over them.** The useful numbers are **viewed vs swiped away** (did they stop) and
  **average percentage viewed** (did they stay).
- Shorts and long-form are judged separately. A bad Short doesn't hurt your long videos. Shorts
  viewers rarely become long-form viewers automatically: a Short helps a channel when it's on the
  same topic as the long-form and points to it.

## TikTok

- The For You feed ranks mainly on how people interact, with watch time weighted heavily, plus
  video information (caption, on-screen text, sounds, hashtags). Follower count matters much less
  than on other platforms, so it's the fastest place for a beginner to get feedback.
- **TikTok is a search engine for younger users.** Say and caption the exact phrase someone would
  type ("best free AI tool for…"). Spoken words and on-screen text are read for search.
- Creator Rewards pays only for original videos over one minute, with follower and view minimums.
  Check the current rules in TikTok's Creator Academy before planning around it.
- Where the numbers live: tap a video → More insights (retention graph, where viewers came from,
  average watch time).

## Instagram Reels

- Instagram's head, Adam Mosseri, has said the main ranking signals are watch time, likes per reach
  and **sends per reach** (people DMing the Reel to a friend), and that sends matter most for
  reaching people who don't follow you. Ask: "would someone send this to a friend?"
- **Originality is enforced.** Accounts that mostly repost others' content get less or no
  recommendation, and videos carrying another app's watermark (e.g. a TikTok logo) are shown
  less. Always upload the clean original file, never a re-download.
- **Trial Reels** show a Reel only to non-followers first. Use them to test a hook without
  putting a flop on your profile.
- Where the numbers live: a Reel → View insights (needs a professional/creator account, free to
  switch in settings).

## Cross-posting one video to all three short platforms

Recommended for a beginner: one vertical video, three uploads, triple the feedback.

- Export one clean master **without** any platform's watermark; upload it natively to each.
- Change the caption per platform: TikTok and YouTube captions should contain search phrases;
  Instagram captions can be more personal.
- Keep on-screen text inside the safe zone (away from the bottom fifth and right edge, where
  buttons and captions sit).
- Use each platform's built-in music/sounds only if you add them in that platform; licensed audio
  doesn't carry across.

## Rules that can get a tech/AI channel demonetized or hidden

- **Inauthentic / mass-produced content.** YouTube doesn't monetize videos that are templated,
  repetitive or mass-produced with little original value; an automated "AI news voiceover over
  stock footage" channel is exactly what this targets. AI is fine as a tool; the creator's own
  demos, opinions and results are the value.
- **Disclose realistic AI media.** YouTube (and the others) require a label when a video contains
  realistic AI-generated or altered people, places or events a viewer could mistake for real.
  AI-written scripts, AI voices on obvious animations, and AI tools shown on screen don't need it.
  Disclosure doesn't reduce reach.
- **Don't reuse other people's footage without adding real commentary**, and never re-upload a
  creator's video, even with credit.
- **Your employer's confidential work** is not content. Show your own side projects, public tools
  and things you're allowed to share.

## Account setup checklist (do once, ~30 minutes)

- [ ] Same handle on all platforms if available (short, easy to say aloud, no numbers if possible)
- [ ] Profile photo: your face or one simple mark, readable at tiny size
- [ ] Bio = the promise from your positioning sentence ("I test AI tools so you don't have to"),
      not a list of job titles
- [ ] Instagram and TikTok switched to a creator/professional account (free; unlocks insights)
- [ ] YouTube channel description written with the words people search for in your niche
- [ ] A notes or folder system for ideas (the `content/` workspace this pack uses)
