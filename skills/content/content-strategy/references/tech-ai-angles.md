# Angles and formats for a tech/AI creator

Read this when choosing the creator's angle and pillars, or when an idea needs a format.

"Tech and AI" is a category, not a niche. Thousands of channels already post "AI news" and "top 5
AI tools". A beginner wins by being **specific about who they help** and by **showing things
nobody else can show**: their own projects, their own tests, their own mistakes.

## Pick an angle (a role the creator plays)

Each angle is a promise to the viewer. Recommend one primary angle; a second can come later.

| Angle | Promise to the viewer | Needs from the creator | Good when they… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The builder** (build in public) | "Watch me build X with AI, including what broke" | A real project they're building; comfort showing unfinished work | Are already building something (check `my-context` projects) |
| **The tester** | "I tried it so you don't have to" | Time to genuinely use tools; honest verdicts | Like trying new tools; can be critical, not just excited |
| **The explainer** | "Understand X in N minutes, no jargon" | Real understanding; ability to simplify; visuals | Have an engineering background and enjoy teaching |
| **The tutor** | "Do X step by step" (searchable how-tos) | Patience; clean screen recordings | Want search traffic that compounds; prefer screen over face |
| **The analyst** | "What this AI news actually means for you" | Fast, accurate reading of primary sources; opinions | Can post quickly and verify claims; enjoy taking positions |
| **The career guide** | "How to get into / grow in tech with AI" | Real experience in the job they talk about | Have a story others want (switching careers, landing roles) |

The builder and tester angles are the strongest starting points for a beginner: they generate
endless ideas from work the creator is doing anyway, and the proof (a demo, a real result) is
built in. "Document, don't create": film the process of what you're already doing.

## Narrow the viewer

"People interested in AI" is not an audience. Push to one specific person:

- ❌ "Developers" → ✅ "Backend developers who want to add AI features without becoming ML experts"
- ❌ "Beginners" → ✅ "Non-technical founders trying to build an MVP with AI coding tools"
- ❌ "Students" → ✅ "CS students who want an AI portfolio project that gets interviews"

Test: could you name three real places this person hangs out online (subreddits, Discords,
newsletters, YouTube channels)? If not, it's still too vague.

## Pillars: three recurring topics

Pillars stop the "what do I post today?" panic. Use three: one that brings new viewers, one
that builds trust, one that's personal.

Example for "the builder" helping backend developers add AI features:

1. **Reach: quick wins.** 30-60 s Shorts: one prompt, one tool trick, one before/after.
2. **Trust: build logs and tutorials.** 8-15 min YouTube videos building a real feature end to end.
3. **Personal: lessons and opinions.** What I got wrong, what I'd do differently, hot takes on new
   releases, grounded in what I actually built.

## Formats that work for tech/AI (and their traps)

| Format | Shape | Trap to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **"I tried X for N days"** | Setup → expectations → what happened → verdict | Ending without a clear verdict |
| **Build log** | Goal → attempt → what broke → fix → result on screen | 3 minutes of setup before anything works |
| **Step-by-step tutorial** | Show the end result first → steps → common error → recap | Assuming viewers have your setup; skipping versions |
| **X vs Y** | Same task, both tools, side by side → winner by use case | "Both are great" (no verdict) |
| **Explainer** | Question → simple analogy → real mechanism → why it matters | Analogies that are wrong in ways experts notice |
| **News breakdown** | What happened (source on screen) → what it means → what to do | Repeating the press release; hype words; being late |
| **Myth vs reality** | Common belief → test it live → result | Straw-manning the belief |
| **Prompt/tool trick** (Short) | Problem → the trick on screen → result | Trick that only works in the creator's setup |
| **Mistakes / lessons** | "I wasted N weeks on X so you don't" | Vague lessons with no specific story |

## Face, voice, or screen only?

All three work for tech. Choose by comfort, because a format the creator dreads won't last:

- **Screen + voice** (faceless): easiest start. Tutorials and demos. Needs a decent microphone more
  than a camera. Text overlays do more work.
- **Face + screen** (picture-in-picture or cutaways): builds trust and a personal brand fastest;
  recommended once they're comfortable.
- **Face only** (talking head): opinions, career, news takes. Needs strong scripting and energy.

Fully automated AI-voice "faceless" channels risk YouTube's inauthentic-content rules and build
no personal brand. Use AI to help write and edit, not to replace the creator.

## Signs an angle is right

- The creator can list 20 video ideas for it in 15 minutes without research.
- They'd watch this channel themselves.
- It connects to their goals in `my-context` (a job, a startup, an audience to sell to later).
- There are channels in the space (proof of demand) but none with this exact viewer + angle combo.
